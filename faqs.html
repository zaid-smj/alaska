A lead-acid battery stores energy chemically. Each cell has positive plates of lead dioxide and negative plates of sponge lead in a sulfuric acid electrolyte. When you draw power, both plates react with the acid and slowly turn into lead sulfate, releasing electricity. Charging reverses the reaction. A 12 V battery has six cells of about 2 V each, connected in series.

How-To Guides

Care instructions from your warranty cardOfficial care for dry-charge and maintenance-free batteries.

Dry-charge batteries

  • Charge at the recommended rate of C10 = Ah ÷ 10.
  • Check the battery's electrolyte level regularly.
  • Use only distilled water to maintain electrolyte levels during service.
  • Always keep the battery surface dry and clean, and apply a layer of petroleum jelly to the terminals to protect them from sulfation.

Maintenance-free (MF) batteries

  • Do not attempt to open the battery or add electrolyte to it.
  • Always keep the battery clean and dry.
  • Do not allow rust or dirt to accumulate on the battery terminals.
  • If the battery is not used for a long period of time, recharge it every 30 days.
Installing a car batteryReplace your car battery safely.

  1. Switch off the engine and all electrics, and remove the key.

  2. Disconnect the negative (−) terminal first, then the positive (+).

  3. Remove the hold-down clamp and lift the old battery out. Batteries are heavy, so lift with care.

  4. Clean the battery tray and cable clamps.

  5. Place the new battery in the same orientation and secure the hold-down clamp.

  6. Connect the positive (+) terminal first, then the negative (−). Never reverse the connection: it can damage the vehicle and voids the warranty.

  7. Tighten the clamps and apply a thin layer of petroleum jelly to the terminals.

  8. Start the engine and check that the lights and charging warning light behave normally.

First charge for a dry-charge batteryWhat happens before a new dry-charge battery goes into service.

  1. The dealer fills the battery with electrolyte at the time of sale.

  2. Initial charging is mandatory before use.

  3. Charge at the recommended C10 rate: the Ah rating divided by 10 (for example, 6.5 A for a 65 Ah battery).

  4. Check the electrolyte level after charging and top up with distilled water if needed.

  5. Make sure the dealer completes, signs and stamps your warranty card.

Jump-starting a car safelyGet going again without damaging either car.

  1. Park the two cars close together without touching, and switch both off.

  2. Connect red to the flat battery's positive (+), then to the good battery's positive (+).

  3. Connect black to the good battery's negative (−), then to bare metal on the flat car's engine, away from the battery.

  4. Start the good car and let it run for a few minutes, then start the flat car.

  5. Remove the cables in reverse order and drive for at least 30 minutes to recharge.

  6. If it keeps happening, have the battery and charging system tested at an Alaska partner.

Connecting a battery to a UPS or solar inverterSet up lead-acid backup correctly.

  1. Choose a solar or deep-cycle model, not a car battery.

  2. Match the system voltage. For 24 V or 48 V systems, connect identical batteries in series.

  3. Use batteries of the same model and age in one bank.

  4. Set the inverter or charge controller to the correct battery type and charging values.

  5. Use cables and fuses sized by a qualified installer, and tighten all connections.

  6. Avoid regular deep discharge, and recharge fully as soon as possible after a power cut.

Installing a Life Series LiFePO4 batteryGet the best from your lithium battery.

  1. Have the system installed by a qualified installer, following the battery manual.

  2. Use a compatible inverter configured for the battery's specifications.

  3. Connect the communication cable (CAN or RS485) on compatible models, so the inverter and battery management system work together.

  4. Do not connect Life Series batteries with other brands or batteries of different capacity.

  5. Keep the installation report and periodic inspection records for warranty support.

Storing a batteryKeep a spare or seasonal battery healthy.

  1. Charge the battery fully before storing it.

  2. Disconnect it and keep it clean, dry and away from heat and direct sun.

  3. Recharge MF batteries every 30 days while they are not in use.

  4. Check dry-charge electrolyte levels before putting the battery back into service.

Checking your battery's healthSpot problems before the battery lets you down.

  1. Watch for warning signs: slow starting, dim lights, short backup time or frequent jump-starts.

  2. Check that the terminals are clean and tight. Loose clamps often look like a dead battery.

  3. Check the voltage when buying an MF battery: if it reads below 12.5 V, the dealer should recharge it.

  4. Have the battery and charging system tested at any Alaska partner if you are unsure.

Recycling your old batteryDispose of lead-acid batteries responsibly.

  1. Do not throw an old battery away or open it.

  2. Keep it upright to avoid leaks, and carry it carefully.

  3. Return it to an authorised dealer for safe handling and responsible recycling.
